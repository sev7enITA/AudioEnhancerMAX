Download questions

Is AudioEnhancerMAX free?

Yes. The direct release is available free under the MIT license. You do not need an AudioEnhancerMAX account for community support or to download the public release.

Does it work offline?

Core audio processing and local transcription run on your Mac once the necessary components and models are available. Models may download on first use. Optional Microsoft Edge Neural TTS sends submitted text to Microsoft; optional Ollama assistance and experimental LAN workers have separate controls. Read the privacy notice before enabling these paths.

Is it available in the Mac App Store?

The verified download here is the direct DMG. The public release notes last recorded the separate 3.8.2/build 3.8.3 App Store submission as Waiting for Review on October 17, 2026. That historical submission status does not confirm present store availability. Apple notarization of the DMG is a separate distribution check.

Does it include every AI model or voice feature?

No. DeepFilterNet, Demucs and speaker diarization depend on their engines and assets being available. Personal Voice Studio is development work and is not promised as a feature of this stable DMG. Consult the status page and your package's runtime capability information.

What has been tested?

The release notes describe packaged-app checks for processing, transcription, speech editing, WAV/MP3/FLAC export, privacy recovery and capsule integrity on a physical Apple Silicon Mac. Transcription used an existing model cache. First-time model download, all macOS versions and perceptual audio quality were outside that validation. Read the exact build evidence.

Where can I get help?

Follow the installation and troubleshooting guide. Community issues are public; use a synthetic or redacted sample instead of uploading a confidential recording.

Before using a result

Listen to the final exported file and check the intended edits. Reports, loudness checks and file hashes support review, but do not certify sound quality, transcription accuracy, speaker consent or compliance. The governance article explains why these distinctions matter.
